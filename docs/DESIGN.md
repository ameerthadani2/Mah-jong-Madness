# Visual design: Tycoon Parlour

Mahjong Mania looks like a Codigames idle-tycoon game (Idle Supermarket Tycoon, Idle Theme Park Tycoon): bright, chunky, illustrated and friendly, and unmistakably a commercial game. Three complete directions were built and compared side by side. Tycoon Parlour won; Jade & Lacquer (deep jade felt, walnut rail, gold leaf) and Neo Arcade (navy glass cards, geometric tiles) were runners-up and are good sources for premium table themes.

Player avatars are the exception to the cartoon style: shaded caricature portraits in the PokerBros vein (see `docs/screenshot-avatars.png`).

Nothing in the UI is an emoji or a system icon. Every icon, avatar, illustration and tile face is drawn with `react-native-svg` or composed Views, so it renders the same on iOS, Android and web.

Every filled shape has the same build: a lighter top tone, the fill, a darker hard "shelf" under it, and a 3px ink outline. Buttons drop onto their shelf when pressed.

## Type

* **Lilita One**: display text and numerals, white with an ink stroke (`StrokeText` layers offset copies behind the fill).
* **Luckiest Guy**: the logo wordmark only. On a Traditional Chinese device the wordmark reads the locale's app name (`common.appName`, 麻將狂熱) in the display face, with the English name small and gold beneath. On the web the page's title is that name too (麻將狂熱 in Traditional Chinese, Mahjong Mania in English), set with the page's `lang` whenever the language is chosen or changed (`i18n/resolve.ts` `applyDocumentLocale`); the app has no router that would set a title of its own.
* **Fredoka** 500–700: UI labels and buttons.
* **Nunito** 600–800: body text.
* **Chinese**: never given a font family, so iOS uses PingFang TC and Android uses Noto Sans CJK at a heavy weight. CJK fonts are not bundled (they would add megabytes).

Fonts load in `App.tsx` behind a branded loading screen; after three seconds the app falls back to system fonts so it never shows a blank screen.

## Tiles

Characters are heavy numerals over a red 萬. Dots are concentric rings in blue, red and green, in the traditional layouts, with an ornate one-dot. Bamboo is stick clusters with node bands; the one-bamboo is a bird on a perch. Winds are navy, 中 red, 發 green, and 白 a blue double frame. Flowers show a blossom, their number and character. Backs are a green gradient with a lattice diamond. Each kind's art is computed once and cached.

## Table

The felt sits inside a chunky wood rail with brass corner studs. A compass in the centre shows the round wind, the tiles left in the wall and each seat's wind, with the active seat lit gold. On tablets and in portrait the wall is drawn around the compass and empties as tiles are drawn. Your hand sits on a wooden rack: one row of 17 in landscape, two rows in portrait. Claims are large pill buttons labelled with the Chinese verb (過 吃 碰 槓 打 胡) and the tiles involved. The coach is an owl with a note: a slim bar on phones, a panel on tablets.

## Display scale (Settings → Display)

Two settings, each Normal / Large / Extra large, saved under their own key (`mjm.display`, see `src/display/`), so the saved Settings shape is unchanged. `DisplayScaleProvider` (wrapping the app in `App.tsx`) offers them through `useDisplayScale()`; without a provider everything is Normal.

| | Normal | Large | Extra large |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tiles (`tileScale`) | 1 | 1.25 | 1.5 |
| Text (`textScale`) | 1 | 1.15 | 1.3 |

* **Tiles** grow in the hand rail, my melds, every pond, the opponents' melds, the result sheet and lesson hands. The geometry is `src/display/tableFit.ts`, pure and unit-tested; at Normal it returns exactly what `components/table/layout.ts` gives.
  * Portrait: the hand still fits 17 tiles at 390 px. At Large and Extra large it wraps into a third row (never more: past three rows the tiles shrink instead). The felt above gives up the height (the order is under Layout rules, Portrait), and when even its least does not fit, the hand steps down a size for the rest of the hand.
  * Landscape: the hand stays one row, at most a fifth of the screen tall, and never scrolls sideways. At Normal my plate and my melds share the rail with it, and a full hand already spans what they leave. My flowers sit over my plate, so that column grows up over the felt's bottom-left corner; the left seat's column ends 4 px above where it starts (measured in the window: `HandRail` `onPlateTop`, `Felt` `leftSeatEnd`), so my flowers never cover its newest discards. At Large and Extra large my plate, melds and flowers move to a row of their own above the hand (as in portrait), which takes that row's height from the felt, so the hand spans the whole width: a full hand's tiles are 45×63 instead of 40×56 at 844×390, 34×48 instead of 29×41 at 667×375 and 55×77 instead of 48×67 at 1024×768 (with melds, fewer hand tiles grow further, up to the height limit). My melds in that row grow up to 32 px and stay on one line beside the plate. The rail keeps a sideways-scroll fallback, with the drawn tile kept in view, should a row ever overflow.
  * Ponds keep at least three tiles a row on a side seat and five in the centre; short landscape phones (under 420 px tall) stop at Large so the side ponds stay on the felt. A side seat's melds are capped so a kong fits its column.
* **Text**: body text (`P` in `components/Ui.tsx`, which lessons, puzzles and the settings cards use) and the result sheet's 台 list scale with `textScale`; headings, buttons, pills and the table's labels do not, since they sit in fixed-size shapes. The OS text size is the floor: React Native already applies `PixelRatio.getFontScale()`, so the app adds only `max(1, textScale / osScale)` (`textFactor` in `src/display/scale.ts`). Picking Normal never undoes a large system font, and the card says so when the OS size is above 100 %.
* Checked with Playwright at 390×844 and 844×390 for every step, and at 667×375, 360×740 and 1024×768 for Large and Extra large.

## Motion and sound

All motion is React Native `Animated` (no extra library); on web it runs without the native driver.

* **Discard**: the new tile slides into its pond from its owner's side (190 ms) and is ringed gold.
* **Call-out**: 吃 碰 槓 胡 自摸 pops up large in the middle of the felt with the caller's name, coloured by call (吃 blue, 碰 green, 槓 purple, 胡 gold, 自摸 red), for about a second; 胡 and 自摸 hold for the 2.2 s before the result sheet. The voice line plays at the same moment; with voice off, or a voice not yet recorded, the banner carries the call alone.
* **Winner reveal**: during that hold the winner's melds and hand are laid out face up under the banner, the concealed tiles flipping over one after another.
* **Result**: the 台 total and every score change count up from zero.
* **Sounds**: draw (my own), discard clack, the clock ticking each of the last five seconds of my move, a tap on selecting a tile or pressing a claim, a fanfare when I win, a falling sigh for 流局. Settings → Sound turns everything off; the voice choice (Mandarin, Taiwanese, off) is separate.

## Accessibility

* Every pressable has a role and a label: shared controls take theirs from their title (or, for icon buttons, `table.a11y.icon.*`); tiles read their name and whether they are selected; claim buttons read the verb and the tiles.
* Touch targets are at least 44 pt, using `hitSlop` where the drawn control is smaller (`slopFor` in `components/table/layout.ts`). The web ignores `hitSlop`, so a round icon button drawn under 44 pt (the table HUD's emotes, coach and exit icons are 40) is padded to 44 with an equal negative margin, which keeps it in place; `npm run e2e:screens` measures the HUD icons. Hand tiles get their extra area above and below, since neighbours sit 2 px apart. The coach's bar (27 px tall on one line) and its panel's "More" are padded the same way on the web. So are the other controls drawn under 44 px (`webPress` in `components/Ui.tsx`): the choice chips (34 px; Home's bot level, and Settings' voice, sizes, language, bot level, rule presets and speeds), which are also drawn at least 44 px wide; the small buttons (42 px, padded 2 px above only, as they often end a scrolling column, where a pixel past its end would make the column scroll and become a Tab stop); the rule editor's values (a 24 px line); the diamond counter (40 px); the result sheet's audit pill (27 px, 20 px in the two-column sheet); and Home's links (20 px), whose press reaches up only to 2 px short of the button above them and the rest below, so a tap on that button's bottom edge stays the button's (`LINK_SLOP` in `logic/homeFit.ts`). The Terms and Privacy links, which end a page or a sheet, are 44 px lines on the web. `npm run e2e:screens` fails when any control on Home (in English and in Traditional Chinese) or anywhere in Settings takes a press under 44 x 44 px, at every size, and when a tap anywhere on the face of a control on Home reaches another control; and the same for the controls of the result sheet.
* No text below 11 px (a unit test scans the table, learning and shared components).
* Dialogs that sit over a screen are modal and named: the Shop's try-on preview is a `dialog` with `aria-modal` and the look's name ("Preview: Jade Parlour table"), `accessibilityViewIsModal` on iOS, a header for its title and a label on every control (Close preview, Buy for 400 diamonds, Play Dumpling, Play again). On the web the Modal itself is the dialog (react-native-web makes its outer element one), so it carries the name and the sheet is not a second dialog; the dimmed scrim behind the sheet closes it on a tap but is hidden from screen readers and is never a focus stop, so the sheet's own close button is the only "Close preview" they reach. `npm run e2e:screens` checks for exactly one named dialog and one reachable close button at every size. Closing it gives focus back to the card's picture that opened it (`display/focus.ts`: the browser's focus on the web, an accessibility focus event on a phone).
* Every modal sheet (the try-on preview, the table's exit sheet, the yes/no questions, the report sheet, and the legal pages) shares two parts, `components/Scrim.tsx`. The scrim is a plain view with a touch responder, not a Pressable: react-native-web gives every Pressable a tabindex, and its Modal's focus trap moves focus to any element that will take it, a tabindex of -1 included. And while a sheet is open on the web, the app's root element is `inert` (`display/inert.ts`), so the page behind it takes no focus or clicks and is hidden from screen readers, including those that ignore `aria-modal`; it comes back when the sheet closes. `npm run e2e:screens` presses Tab ten times and Shift+Tab four times in the preview, the exit sheet and the replace-table question, and fails if focus leaves the dialog's own controls or the page is not inert while it is open, or is still inert after it closes.
* Suits carry a small ink mark in the top-right corner (萬 square, 筒 ring, 條 bar) so they never depend on colour alone. It is drawn on tiles 26 px and wider.

## Known gaps

* Only verified in the web build; native SVG text, gradients and PingFang weights still need a device check.
* Every tile is an SVG; profile on a low-end Android phone before launch.
* The wall around the compass is decorative and does not track the dead wall.
* Hand tiles in portrait are about 36 px wide (17 tiles across two rows on a 360 px phone); their touch area reaches 44 pt only vertically, and only on a phone (hitSlop). On the web export the smallest hand tile measures 29x41 px at 667x375, 34x48 at 360x740 and 37x52 at 390x844 (logged by `npm run e2e:screens`).
* Large and Extra large text scale body text only; headings and buttons keep their size.
