# Fair Play Charter

Mahjong Mania exists because players are tired of card and tile apps where the deal feels scripted. This document is the promise, the mechanism, exactly what each player's phone can check, and what it cannot.

## The promise

1. **Real luck only.** The tiles you draw are determined by a shuffle seed built from five secret shares: yours, the other players' and the table server's. When your phone watched the whole deal in one attempt and its share went into the seed (a **Verified** hand), nobody (another player, a club owner, the server or us) could have picked the seed after seeing your share. When it could not watch the whole deal, the hand says so in amber. There is no "luck balancing", no engagement tuning, no rubber-banding, no hidden player tiers, no streak engine. The engine has exactly one source of randomness and it is auditable.
2. **Same rules for everyone.** The rules engine is deterministic and the same code runs on every device. The host validates every action with the same function your phone uses to show you your legal moves.
3. **Every hand can be checked.** After every hand, every player's device re-derives the shuffle, replays the hand from the published log, and checks the log against every move it sent and every table state it was shown. Hands are checked as a chain, from the first deal to the final scores. If anything does not match, the app says so on the result screen, in red.
4. **Nothing in the shop touches the table.** Purchases are cosmetic, coaching or club ownership. No item reveals information, changes odds or buys time that other players do not get.

## The mechanism

```
before the hand              server publishes                  after the hand
───────────────              ────────────────                  ──────────────
seat 0 picks s0       ──▶  H(s0) ┐                         ──▶  s0 revealed
seat 1 picks s1       ──▶  H(s1) │                         ──▶  s1 revealed
seat 2 picks s2       ──▶  H(s2) ├─ all five to every seat ──▶  s2 revealed
seat 3 picks s3       ──▶  H(s3) │  before any reveal      ──▶  s3 revealed
server picks s4       ──▶  H(s4) ┘                         ──▶  s4 revealed
                 seed = H(mjm-seed-v2:match:hand:0=s0|1=s1|2=s2|3=s3|4=s4)
                 wall = shuffle(144 tiles, seed)
```

* `H` is SHA-256. Your phone reveals its secret only after it holds all five commitments for that hand, so nobody can pick a secret after seeing yours. A server that asks for your secret first is recorded and the hand fails its audit. The commitments are published once per attempt: a server that sends a different set for the same attempt (for instance a new s4 picked after seeing your secret) fails the audit; an identical re-send, as a returning player receives, is fine.
* The seed takes exactly five shares, one for each of seats 0–4, each labelled with its seat (`mjm-seed-v2` marks this derivation). Secrets are lowercase hex. An audit that lists an extra, missing or repeated share, or a seat outside 0–4, fails, so the server cannot slip in a sixth share chosen after the reveals.
* Each human player's phone picks its own secret. The server picks its own share (s4), the shares of bot seats and any stand-in share, so those are only as unpredictable as the server is honest.
* The wall is produced by a SHA-256 counter-mode generator keyed by the whole 256-bit seed (`mjm-sha256ctr-v1`, stamped on every audit so old hands stay verifiable after upgrades) driving a Fisher–Yates shuffle with rejection sampling. Bots use a separate, non-security PRNG for tie-breaks. Secrets come from the platform's secure random generator; the app refuses to deal without one.
* A player who misses the commit window (late, or away) gets a stand-in share from the server for that attempt; a player who returns is told so before anything else about the hand. A player who commits but does not reveal makes the ceremony restart, and the server supplies that seat's share in every later attempt at the same hand, so each human seat is blamed at most once and an honest table needs at most one plus the number of human seats attempts. Every restart is published in the audit as `{attempt, seats, cause}`, and every commit request carries its attempt number.
* Your phone records, attempt by attempt, which attempts it was asked to commit to, whether it revealed and whether it was stood in. It rejects an audit that reports fewer attempts than it was asked to commit to, an attempt number it was sent twice or skipped while its link was up, restarts that blame a bot or anything but a player seat, blame a seat a second time, or need more attempts than an honest table could. When it watched every attempt from the first without losing its link, the count must match exactly and a restart that blames nobody fails (only a server that crashed restarts that way, and that drops every link). A restart that blames your seat for an attempt you revealed in fails unless the server then stood you in, and so does any deal that used a fresh share from you after blaming you, whether or not your link held. A server that re-deals after seeing your secret therefore either fails your audit or must stand you in.
* **Green only for a deal your phone watched whole, in one attempt.** A hand is at most amber, **Logged — the server could have picked this deal**, with the reason, when its deal was restarted, when the deal went on while your phone's link was down, when you joined mid-hand, when the server made your share, when the hand was played while your phone was away, when turns were played again after a server restart, or when the app was restarted around the deal. Every other check still runs; a failed check is red whatever the colour would have been.
* **One record per table, across reconnects and restarts.** The app keeps one table client for as long as you sit at a table: a dropped link rebinds it to the new connection, and an app restart restores its saved record (ceremony record, chain, frozen bot set, the moves it sent, the states it was shown, the last 50 audits) from the phone's storage. A server that drops your link to make your phone forget what it saw gains nothing. The record is saved a moment after each change, so a restarted app cannot be sure it holds the last thing it did before it stopped (it may already have revealed its secret for the next deal). It therefore does not vouch for the first deal it hears of after a restart: that hand is amber (*the app was restarted around this deal*), and the one after it can be green again. An app that comes back with only its rejoin token and no record is treated the same way, and if it sits down part-way through a match, the scores its first hand starts from are marked as unchecked.
* **The log is bound to your own moves.** Your phone keeps a ledger of every move it sent with the turn of the view it acted on; the server echoes the turn and a reason code when it refuses one. Each log entry says whether the seat's own player chose it (`player`) or the table did (`auto`: a bot seat, a player who is away, a clock that ran out). The audit fails if a move credited to you is not one you sent, or changes one you sent; if the table moved for you while your link was up and your time had not run out; or if a move the table made for you passed up a legal win (the table's own play always takes one). Your phone also checks every table state it was shown against its replay of the log.
* **The clock is the rules' clock, not the server's word.** How long you have for a move is worked out by your phone from the table's rules: the turn clock, plus your time bank, which starts each hand at the rules' value and shrinks only by what your own earlier moves used beyond the turn clock. The deadline and time-bank fields the server sends can only add time to that, never take it away, so a server that claims your bank is already spent, or sets a deadline a moment away, and then moves for you fails the audit. How much time actually passed is read from the server's time stamps, and your phone believes those only as far as its own clock allows: the server cannot have answered a move, or dealt from your secret, before your phone sent it, so from any such fixed point on, the server's clock may not gain more than 5 seconds on your phone's over one connection. Before the first fixed point on a connection the allowance is 30 seconds, for network delay. Phone clocks that are hours off, slow links, a sleeping phone and reconnects mid-turn are not flagged. Two things your phone cannot know after a lost link, and takes on the server's word: how much of your time bank was used while it was away, and whether your time ran out during a turn in which the link dropped.
* **The table may not turn your moves away.** The server refuses only what is illegal in the state it is in, and every state it reached before a refusal has reached your phone first. At the audit your phone replays the hand and checks each refusal: if the move was legal in the state the server's own frames showed when it refused, the hand fails. A legal move that your phone sent over an unbroken link more than 30 seconds before the table made its own move for that turn, and that the table never answered, fails too. A move that reaches the table after your time ran out is honestly played on your next turn if it is still legal, and is not flagged.
* **Anchors.** The first hand of a match must start exactly where a new match starts under the table's rules (derived from the rules, not assumed). For a phone that sat down in the lobby this holds for the first hand it is dealt, whatever number the server gives it. The bot seats and the rules are frozen at the match's first commit request: a later change of who is a bot, or of the rules, is flagged, and the restart bound and the bot check use the frozen set. A `welcome` that moves you to another seat or another match while the match is on is flagged and your seat stays where it was; a new match id is accepted only after the hands your phone verified say the match is over.
* If the server cannot agree a deal (a fault on its side), it says so and returns to the lobby; a seated player's start, or the next tick while a player is still connected, deals the hand again. Players who saw the abandoned attempts will see that hand fail its audit.
* **Hands chain, one at a time.** After verifying a hand, your phone works out where it leaves the match (scores, dealer, dealer streak, rotations, prevailing wind, next hand number). The next deal must be that hand and start from exactly that state. From the moment your phone is asked to commit to a hand, or is shown it, until that hand's result arrives, the table has no business with any other hand: a commit request, state or result for another hand number fails the audit, so the server cannot drop a deal it does not like by moving on to the next number. A server that rewrites the scores between hands (also while your phone is reconnecting or restarting), skips a hand or deals a finished hand again fails the audit. A result re-sent to a returning player must be the same audit, result and scores as before: a different one fails and never replaces what your phone verified, and an audit under another match id is treated as tampering.
* **Hands you missed.** While your link is down the table plays on, with the table moving for your seat. When your phone returns it tells the server which hand it last saw end, and the server sends the audit of every hand that ended since. Your phone checks each one as far as a device that was not there can: the shares and the seed, a replay that starts where the last hand it verified left the match, the restart accounting, and whatever it did see of the hand before it left (its own share, the states it was shown, the moves it sent). A hand that passes is amber (*your phone was away when this hand ended*) and the chain carries on through it, so the hand now on the table is checked as usual. Records that do not replay, do not follow from the last verified hand or skip a hand fail, and the hand on the table fails with them; a hand filed as "missed" for a phone that never left fails too. If the server sends no records at all, the next hand is amber with the reason that the scores it started from could not be checked. A dishonest server could use that gap to change the scores; the amber note is the most your phone can say about it.
* **The end of a match.** Your phone believes a `match_over` only when the last hand it verified ends the match under the rules and the final scores are exactly the ones the chain gives. Anything else is refused and shown in red, and the match stays pinned: the server cannot end a match early, invent final scores, or announce a fake end followed by a "new match" to make your phone forget a deal or a score. This holds after a dropped link as well, because an honest server first sends the hands you missed. Only a phone with no chain to follow (it came back without its record, or was never shown the hands it missed) shows the end without being able to check it, and the Fair play screen then says the final scores could not be checked.
* **A server restart.** The server saves each table a moment after the fact, so after a crash it may resume a hand a few turns back, and those turns are played again, not necessarily the same way. A restored table tells every returning player which hand it resumed and at which turn (or that it resumed before the deal). Your phone then drops what it had been shown beyond that point, once per hand, and only what had reached it over a connection that has since been lost (a restart drops every connection). The hand is amber: *the server restarted during this hand and some turns were played again*. An orderly restart that lost nothing stays green. Turns played again without the notice, over a connection that never dropped, or after a second, different notice for the same hand fail, as any rewind does. A dishonest server could stage one such restart per hand to take back a few turns; the hand is then amber, never green.
* The audit (`seed`, all commitments, all secrets, the restarts, the full action log, the final state hash) is sent to every player. The client checks that it was asked to commit for this hand, that its own commitment and secret were recorded unchanged, that the commitments match the ones it was shown, that there is exactly one commitment and one secret per seat 0–4, every reveal against its commitment, the seed, a replay of the log through the engine (final hash, announced result and scores, its own final hand), the chaining, clock and refusal checks above and the ceremony accounting above.

## What your phone can and cannot check

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody picked the seed after seeing your share | Checked by your phone for each hand whose deal it watched whole, in one attempt, with its own share in the seed (**Verified**). Otherwise (a restart of the deal, a dropped link during the deal, a share the server made for you, joining mid-hand, a hand played while you were away, an app restart around the deal) the hand shows amber **Logged — the server could have picked this deal** and this claim rests on the server. |
| The server did not keep re-dealing until it liked the deal | Every restart makes the hand amber, and restarts are checked against what the human seats could have caused and against what your phone saw itself, across reconnects and app restarts. A re-deal that hides behind a dropped link or forged accounting fails; on a phone that came back without its saved record it cannot be shown to be a re-deal, so that first deal is amber. The server can still claim that other players' connections dropped (it cannot be told from a real drop), which is why such hands are amber, not green. |
| The server did not change the scores or the dealer between hands | Checked: the first hand must start where a new match starts, and each later hand where the previous hand, as replayed, left the match. The exception is stated on the hand: after hands your phone missed and was shown no records of, the starting scores are marked as unchecked (amber). |
| The server did not skip a hand, deal one twice or abandon a deal by moving on | Checked: your phone is on one hand until its result arrives, and the next hand must be the one the last verified hand leads to. |
| The match ended when the rules say, with the scores the hands give | Checked: an end the verified hands do not lead to is refused and shown in red, and a new match id is accepted only after a verified end. A phone that holds no chain of hands (no saved record, or missed hands it was shown no records of) shows the end as unchecked. |
| The server applied the rules correctly | Checked: replaying the published log must reproduce the final state, the announced result, the scores and every state your phone was shown. |
| The server did not change or drop your moves | Checked: every move credited to you must be one you sent; a move the server refused must have been illegal in the state it showed; a legal move left unanswered for more than 30 seconds and replaced by the table's own fails. **Not detectable:** a move you sent in the last 30 seconds before your time ran out and that the server claims never arrived looks the same as a slow network. |
| The server did not play your turn before your time ran out | Checked against the rules' turn clock and your time bank as your phone tracked it, not against the clock fields the server sends; a move the table makes for you must also never pass up a win. **Limit:** the server's time stamps are believed up to 5 seconds beyond your phone's own clock (30 seconds before your first move or reveal on a connection), so a turn could be cut short by that much without failing; after a reconnect your phone takes the server's word for what is left of your time bank, and nothing is checked for a turn during which your link dropped. |
| Hands played while you were away were dealt and scored honestly | Partly checked: the server's record of each missed hand must have matching shares and seed, replay under the rules and chain from the last hand your phone verified. Your phone did not watch the deal or the play, so these hands are amber. |
| A hand replayed after a server crash is the hand you saw | Not fully checkable: the turns played again replace what your phone was shown. The hand is amber with the reason, once per hand and only with the server's notice and a lost connection; anything else fails. |
| The server did not look at the wall during the hand | **Not checked.** The server knows the seed from the deal onwards. Mitigation: the planned cryptographic shuffle (below). |
| The server did not leak the wall to another player | **Not checked.** The server sees every wall by design and could pass one to a player. Mitigations this release: one seat per account at a table (a second seat for the same account is refused, so one account cannot see two hands), and quick match never seats two players who have blocked each other. Nothing in the audit can show a leak. |
| Another player did not see your hand | Enforced by the server: each seat receives only its own view (no other hand, no other seat's drawn tile, claim options or concealed kong; while a discard can be claimed the table lists every other seat as still to answer, so it never shows who could pong, kong or win on it). This relies on the server being honest; nothing in the audit shows it. Two players colluding over another channel are not detected. |

## Publishing and testing the generator

In priority order: a plain-language and technical spec page (this document plus `packages/engine/src/rng.ts`); a downloadable per-hand audit from the result screen; a standalone open-source verifier (CLI and browser) that rebuilds the wall from an audit, in the spirit of Tenhou's public wall reproducers; the engine and protocol under an OSI licence; and a monthly tile-statistics report (per-tile draw frequencies, dealer win rate, win-type distribution with chi-square p-values across public hands). Before buying a certificate, run and publish the open batteries on the DRBG output (Dieharder, NIST SP 800-22, TestU01 Crush/BigCrush) plus a GLI-style "range 1–144, 144 selections" draw file; then budget a GLI-19-track RNG certificate (4–8 weeks, covers the RNG/shuffle only) once revenue or any prize context justifies it.

## Roadmap to zero-trust dealing

* **Mental mahjong shuffle** (later, opt-in "paranoid mode"): elliptic-curve SRA so every draw is decrypted cooperatively and no device, host included, learns a tile before its owner. Cost is roughly 430 point multiplications and ~55 KB per player per hand plus a round trip per draw, and no known protocol tolerates a player dropping mid-hand — which conflicts with crash-resume. Ship only once key escrow for disconnected players is solved. Until then online tables run on the game server, which is trusted not to look at the wall; offline games against bots run on your phone.
* **Third-party RNG and engine audit**: publish the engine, the seed derivation and a statistical report on millions of simulated hands (`npm run sim`) so an independent lab can certify the distribution. Simulation today: win rates by seat within noise of 25% each, draw rate ~4%, no seat bias.
* **Signed audits**: hosts sign each audit so disputes can be settled offline by anyone with the public key.

## What players see

* A green **✓ Shuffle & log verified** badge on every result screen when your phone watched the whole deal in one attempt with your own share in it; amber **Logged — the server could have picked this deal** (no shield) with the reason when everything checked out but the server could have chosen the deal or your phone did not watch all of the hand (a restart of the deal, the app or the server, a dropped link, hands played while you were away); red **⚠ Audit failed** with the reason if anything is off.
* A **Fair play** screen listing each hand's seed, commitments, secrets, action count and hash, for the last 50 hands at this table (kept across reconnects and app restarts).
* Planned, not yet true: the engine and the protocol published under an open licence, with a standalone verifier (see "Publishing and testing the generator"). No licence has been chosen yet and all rights are reserved (`docs/OWNER_TODO.md`).
