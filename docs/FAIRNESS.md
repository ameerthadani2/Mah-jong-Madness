# Fair Play Charter

Mahjong Mania exists because players are tired of card and tile apps where the deal feels scripted. This document is the promise, the mechanism, exactly what each player's phone can check, and what it cannot.

## The promise

1. **Real luck only.** The tiles you draw are determined by a shuffle seed built from five secret shares: yours, the other players' and the table server's. When your phone watched the whole deal in one attempt and its share went into the seed (a **Verified** hand), nobody (another player, a club owner, the server or us) could have picked the seed after seeing your share. When it could not watch the whole deal, the hand says so in amber. There is no "luck balancing", no engagement tuning, no rubber-banding, no hidden player tiers, no streak engine. The engine has exactly one source of randomness and it is auditable.
2. **Same rules for everyone.** The rules engine is deterministic and the same code runs on every device. The host validates every action with the same function your phone uses to show you your legal moves.
3. **Every hand can be checked.** After every hand, every player's device re-derives the shuffle, replays the hand from the published log, and checks the log against every move it sent and every table state it was shown. Hands are checked as a chain, from the first deal to the final scores. If anything does not match, the app says so on the result screen, in red.
4. **Nothing in the shop touches the table.** Purchases are cosmetic, coaching or club ownership. No item reveals information, changes odds or buys time that other players do not get.

## The mechanism

```
before the hand              server publishes                  after the hand
───────────────              ────────────────                  ──────────────
seat 0 picks s0       ──▶  H(s0) ┐                         ──▶  s0 revealed
seat 1 picks s1       ──▶  H(s1) │                         ──▶  s1 revealed
seat 2 picks s2       ──▶  H(s2) ├─ all five to every seat ──▶  s2 revealed
seat 3 picks s3       ──▶  H(s3) │  before any reveal      ──▶  s3 revealed
server picks s4       ──▶  H(s4) ┘                         ──▶  s4 revealed
                 seed = H(mjm-seed-v2:match:hand:0=s0|1=s1|2=s2|3=s3|4=s4)
                 wall = shuffle(144 tiles, seed)
```

* `H` is SHA-256. Your phone reveals its secret only after it holds all five commitments for that hand, so nobody can pick a secret after seeing yours. A server that asks for your secret first is recorded and the hand fails its audit. The commitments are published once per attempt: a server that sends a different set for the same attempt (for instance a new s4 picked after seeing your secret) fails the audit; an identical re-send, as a returning player receives, is fine. Commitments and stand-in notices are sent only for the hand being dealt: one for another hand fails the hand your phone is on and replaces nothing it holds, and a malformed set fails that hand whatever hand it names. Your phone keeps the set of each hand under that hand, and once it has revealed against a set, only a new attempt at that hand's deal (which the audit accounts for) replaces it: a set or stand-in notice for a later hand, which a returning player may honestly be sent after hands it missed, is kept under that later hand and leaves the set of the hand it revealed against as it was, so the audit of that hand is held to the commitments it saw before it revealed. Such a frame also says the hand your phone was on has ended: an honest server deals the next hand only then, and sends nothing more about that hand to a player who missed its end. So a frame about that hand that comes after it (its state, its end, its commitments, a reveal request, a clock frame) fails that hand, until your phone moves on to another hand or the server says it restarted.
* The seed takes exactly five shares, one for each of seats 0–4, each labelled with its seat (`mjm-seed-v2` marks this derivation). Secrets are lowercase hex. An audit that lists an extra, missing or repeated share, or a seat outside 0–4, fails, so the server cannot slip in a sixth share chosen after the reveals.
* Each human player's phone picks its own secret. The server picks its own share (s4), the shares of bot seats and any stand-in share, so those are only as unpredictable as the server is honest.
* The wall is produced by a SHA-256 counter-mode generator keyed by the whole 256-bit seed (`mjm-sha256ctr-v1`, stamped on every audit so old hands stay verifiable after upgrades) driving a Fisher–Yates shuffle with rejection sampling. Bots use a separate, non-security PRNG for tie-breaks. Secrets come from the platform's secure random generator; the app refuses to deal without one.
* A player who misses the commit window (late, or away) gets a stand-in share from the server for that attempt; a player who returns is told so before anything else about the hand. A player who commits but does not reveal makes the ceremony restart, and the server supplies that seat's share in every later attempt at the same hand, so each human seat is blamed at most once and an honest table needs at most one plus the number of human seats attempts. Every restart is published in the audit as `{attempt, seats, cause}`, and every commit request carries its attempt number.
* Your phone records, attempt by attempt, which attempts it was asked to commit to, whether it revealed and whether it was stood in. It rejects an audit that reports fewer attempts than it was asked to commit to, an attempt number it was sent twice or skipped while its link was up, restarts that blame a bot or anything but a player seat, blame a seat a second time, or need more attempts than an honest table could. When it watched every attempt from the first without losing its link, the count must match exactly and a restart that blames nobody fails (only a server that crashed restarts that way, and that drops every link). A restart that blames your seat for an attempt you revealed in fails unless the server then stood you in, and so does any deal that used a fresh share from you after blaming you, whether or not your link held. A server that re-deals after seeing your secret therefore either fails your audit or must stand you in.
* **Green only for a deal your phone watched whole, in one attempt.** A hand is at most amber, **Logged — the server could have picked this deal**, with the reason, when its deal was restarted, when the deal went on while your phone's link was down, when you joined mid-hand, when the server made your share, when the hand was played while your phone was away, when the table played for your seat while your connection was down, when one of your turns reached your phone late, when turns were played again after a server restart, or when the app was restarted around the deal. Every other check still runs; a failed check is red whatever the colour would have been.
* **One record per table, across reconnects and restarts.** The app keeps one table client for as long as you sit at a table: a dropped link rebinds it to the new connection, and an app restart restores its saved record (ceremony record, chain, frozen bot set, the moves it sent, the states it was shown, the table's answers to its timing checks, the last 50 audits) from the phone's storage. A server that drops your link to make your phone forget what it saw gains nothing, and neither does one that says the seat was opened on another device so that you resume the table from Home. The record also keeps how far your phone was holding the hand under way to the moves it sent: after a restart, or a resume from Home, every move credited to you before the latest state the record holds must still be one your phone sent. **Limit:** a move credited to you at that state's turn or later, before the restored app is shown the table again, is not checked against your moves. The app saves its record 0.4 seconds after each change, so such a move may be one it sent in the moment before it stopped, and nothing your phone holds tells it from one the server made in your name while the app was not running (you had put it away, say, or the phone closed it). Each state is saved with a check figure worked out from all its figures. A restored state whose check figure still matches is judged as it was when your phone saw it, however far its figures lie from what an honest server sends: a turn shown with a cut clock (a deadline a day before its stamp included), or held back on a connection your phone held, still fails its hand after a restart, whether the table played that turn for you or you played it yourself. Damaged storage can change any figure (one damaged digit can move a time stamp or a deadline), so of a restored state whose check figure does not match, or one from a record saved before states carried one, what the record says of how late it reached your phone is kept only when it holds together (each figure in range and consistent with the others), and is otherwise judged as an app that kept no such figures would judge it; such a state (saved again without a check figure, so it stays one however often the app restarts) is never failed for having shown you your turn late on a connection your phone held, whether by the watch's measure or by its own stamp and deadline: that makes its hand amber, not red. Nor is it compared with the replay, or with a state shown again for the same turn: one damaged digit of its hash would otherwise fail the hand. It gives no fixed point between the two clocks, nor a figure the next hands are measured by, and a check of a move the table made for you that could rest on it (whether your time had run out, what was taken from your time bank) makes its hand amber instead of red, with the reason (*the app restarted with a damaged record of this hand*). Storage that damages the record never makes a hand red, and a server cannot damage it. The record is saved a moment after each change (changes that keep coming, such as a seat list the server re-sends every few hundred milliseconds, do not put the save off), and at once when the server says the seat was opened on another device, before the table screen goes, whatever the server's welcome says of the room: a welcome that names another room changes nothing, and one that leaves the rejoin token out is malformed and taken as nothing (below), so neither can stop the app keeping the record, nor make a resume from Home pick up an older one; still, a restarted app cannot be sure it holds the last thing it did before it stopped (it may already have revealed its secret for the next deal). It therefore does not vouch for the first deal it hears of after a restart: that hand is amber (*the app was restarted around this deal*), and the one after it can be green again. An app that comes back with only its rejoin token and no record is treated the same way, and if it sits down part-way through a match, the scores its first hand starts from are marked as unchecked. The app joins a table it holds a record of with that record's token however you open the table (Resume on Home, a notification, a club's table, Join by code, a quick match that sends you back to your seat), so it carries on with its record. On a phone that holds no record of the table your account still gets its own seat back from the server, without the token, and that phone is treated like one that came back with its token alone: the first hand it is shown is amber, with the scores it starts from unchecked. Such a phone did not watch the moves credited to your seat in that hand before it was first shown the hand, so, like the moves of a hand it missed, they keep the end of the match from being shown as verified. **Cost:** a player who moves to another device during the first hand of a match, after making a move, sees the end of the match as unchecked on that device (from the second hand on, the scores the new device starts from are unchecked anyway). The app does not throw a table's record away when the table leaves Home: when the server says the table is gone (it ended, or was closed while you were away), when you give it up, or when another table takes its place, the record is kept for a day under the game server, the room and the match (the newest three). Home does not offer such a table for Resume. If your account is then seated at that room again by any path (a quick match that sends you back to your seat or matches you into that room, a club's table, a notification, Join by code), the app joins with the kept record's token and says which hands it holds, and takes the record up again as a restart takes up the saved one, but only when the table's welcome names the record's match. So a server that says a table is gone and then seats your account there again finds your phone holding what it checked: a move it forged in your name before is red, and the hands it played meanwhile come as missed hands. A welcome that names another match is another table under the same code (codes are used again once a table is gone): there the record is not taken up, the phone goes on as one that joined with nothing, and the records that table sends in answer to the hands it said it held are not judged. **Limit:** a server that seats your account again under another match id keeps the kept record from being taken up; the hand that record was on then never gets a verdict, as when a server stops a table for good, and the hand your phone is shown under the new match id fails its checks unless it was dealt under that id. **Limit:** a phone that has just sat down with neither a token nor a record cannot tell a seat its account took back part-way through a match from a seat in the lobby, so a server that deals such a phone a later hand first makes that hand amber (its starting scores unchecked), not red.
* **The log is bound to your own moves.** Your phone keeps a ledger of every move it sent with the turn of the view it acted on; the server echoes the turn and a reason code when it refuses one. Once your phone is asked to commit to a hand it holds the seat from before the deal, so the ledger holds for that hand from the deal (turn 0) on, whether or not the table shows you the hand: a server that deals from your share, keeps the table from your screen and plays your moves itself fails that hand. Each log entry says whether the seat's own player chose it (`player`) or the table did (`auto`: a bot seat, a player who left, a clock that ran out). Each entry also names the turn it was made on, and must name the turn the replay has reached: every check of your own moves is keyed by that number, so an entry numbered otherwise fails the hand. The audit fails if a move credited to you is not one you sent, or changes one you sent; if the table moved for you before your time had run out, with your link up or down; or if a move the table made for you passed up a legal win (the table's own play always takes one). Your phone also checks every table state it was shown against its replay of the log: the state's hash, and the view of the table it showed you. The hash covers tiles your seat may not see, so it cannot tell what you were shown; the view itself (your hand, every meld and discard, whose turn it was, the tile you drew, what you could claim, the scores) must be exactly the replayed state as your seat sees it, and so must the view the result screen shows. Your phone keeps a short digest of each view (SHA-256, 128 bits), saved with its record, so a server that hides a win from your screen, or shows a tile you do not hold, fails the hand while its hashes match. Working the digests out and comparing them costs about 15 ms per hand on a desktop computer, half of it as the states arrive and half at the audit. The table shows one turn with one view: a second copy of a turn that shows another view than the first, with the same hash, fails the hand when it comes, as one with another hash does. Your phone keeps up to 600 states of each hand, several times what an honest table sends. When more come, a copy of a turn it already holds on the same connection that adds nothing its checks read gives way first (the first copy and the latest of each turn on each connection stay, and so does the copy that showed the latest end of your clock), and no view or hash you were shown gives way while anything else can; a hand whose record ran over at all is amber at best, with the reason.
* **What the table plays is fixed by a published rule, and your phone recomputes it.** For a seat whose clock ran out, or that is away after its link dropped, the table takes a win when one is offered, passes any other claim on another seat's discard, and otherwise plays the bot policy at Club strength or above (Pro at a Pro table) from the seed `timeout:<seat>`. A bot seat, and a seat whose player left on purpose, plays the bot policy at the table's strength from the seed `<match id>:bot<seat>`. The bot policy is deterministic given the state, the seat and the seed, so at the audit your phone replays the hand and recomputes every such move on the state just before it (a bot's delay timer does not matter: the log order is the order the moves were made in). A move the table made for you must be the timeout rule's, or a bot's in a stretch your phone left the table on purpose; a bot's move must be its policy's; a move the table made for another player must be one of the two. Anything else fails the hand: a Pong forced on a timed-out player, a discard into an opponent's win, a bot that dodges a discard it would have thrown because it has seen your hand. The server says how strong its bots are when you sit down, and your phone pins that from the match's first deal, like the bot seats; a change mid-match fails the hand. A server that does not say so fails every hand in which it made a move for a bot or for a seat out of time (no host was ever deployed without it, so there is no older server to make room for). Only a phone restored from a save written before it kept the strength cannot know what the server said; for the rest of that match such hands are amber, with the reason. Checking costs about 20 ms per hand on a desktop.
* **The clock is the rules' clock, not the server's word.** How long you have for a move is worked out by your phone from the table's rules: the turn clock, plus your time bank, which starts each hand at the rules' value and shrinks only by what your own earlier moves used beyond the turn clock. The deadline and time-bank fields the server sends can only add time to that, never take it away, so a server that claims your bank is already spent, or sets a deadline a moment away, and then moves for you fails the audit. On a connection your phone already held, the state that starts your turn must also give you the whole turn clock by its own deadline and time stamp, and the table's clock never steps back: a server whose wall clock is set back keeps real time. How much time actually passed is read from the server's time stamps, and your phone believes those only as far as its own clock allows: the server cannot have answered a move, or dealt from your secret, before your phone sent it (the first time it sent it: a server that asks for the secret again, on the same connection or a new one, does not move that time), so from any such fixed point on, the server's clock may not gain more than 5 seconds on your phone's. That holds for as long as the app runs, across reconnects and from hand to hand: the server's clock does not change when a socket does. A restart of the app, or a resume of the table from Home, starts afresh: the phone's clock may have been set anywhere while the app was closed, so what was learned before it fails a hand only by the states shown before it, and what is learned after it only by the states shown after it. A move the table made for you that only the other side of the restart calls early makes the hand amber, with the reason, not red: a server whose clock leapt just after the restart looks the same as a phone clock set back while the app was closed. Before the first fixed point of a match the allowance is 30 seconds, for network delay. Phone clocks that are hours off, a phone clock set back or forward while the app is closed (at most the hand under way at the restart turns amber), slow links, a sleeping phone and reconnects mid-turn are not flagged.
* **A dropped link does not cost you your turn.** A seat whose connection drops is not played like a bot. It keeps its turn clock and its time bank exactly as if it were connected, and if you are back before they run out the move is still yours. Only when the clock runs out does the table move for the seat (a win is taken, any other offer on a discard is passed, your own turn is played as a bot would play it), and from then on it plays the seat without waiting until you return. A player who leaves the table on purpose, or is removed from it, is played at once. Your phone holds the server to this on its own clock. It knows when the last frame before the drop arrived and when the first one after the reconnect did, timed on its monotonic clock (the one that only moves forward), so setting the phone's time back, a network time correction or a time zone trip while you are away cannot turn an honest timeout red, and a table that played you inside a one-second drop still fails. A phone's monotonic clock can stand still while the phone sleeps, so when the wall clock has run more than a second further between two readings, your phone counts the wall clock's time instead, and an honest timeout while your phone slept stays amber. A wall clock that jumps forward during a drop is counted the same way: that can turn a failure into amber, never into green, and a server cannot make your phone's clock jump. Wall time alone is used only across an app restart, where no monotonic clock survives. The first move the table made for you during the drop needs a whole clock to have run out in that time: what was left of the move you owed, or a whole turn clock plus your time bank when the turn came while you were gone. If the time is too short the hand fails. If it is long enough the hand is amber, with the reason that the table played for you while your connection was down (your phone did not watch those moves). After a reconnect the time bank the server states may not be lower than the bank your phone had tracked less the time you were gone, and a move you owe in the first frame cannot have been on the clock for longer than that either. After the app restarts, or the table is resumed from Home, and until you move again, your phone measures the time you were gone by what it learned of the server's clock before the restart. The phone's own clock may have been set while the app was closed, so a bank stated lower than that measure allows makes the hand amber with the reason, not red, and never green. Where your phone has no measure of the time (you left the table yourself, your seat was opened on another device, or the app was restarted in between) the table's moves for you are noted in amber and not judged. Your phone learns that the seat was opened on another device from the server's notice, or, when that notice was lost on a stalled connection, from the server when the phone comes back on its rejoin token and takes the seat back: it then stops as if the notice had come, and you resume the table from Home. From the notice on, your phone takes nothing more from that connection and sends no move on it, so a server that goes on sending the table there after the notice gets no verdict from it; the connection you resume on is measured as any other. That costs the time until you resume, also when the notice is true: the table's moves for you meanwhile are noted in amber, and a turn that started meanwhile counts as one that reached your phone late (amber). Only the server's first answer on a new connection can say so; said later on the same connection, it is ignored. Either way the notice excuses nothing before the last state your phone was shown while it held the seat: a move the log credits to you before that state must still be one your phone sent, since an honest server stops sending to a connection once another device takes its seat. Your phone keeps every such stretch of the hand however many notices come (two that touch become one), so a server that sends the notice again and again, each time you resume, cannot push out the stretch that holds a forged move. A server that says so falsely gains nothing it could not get by sending the notice. **Limit:** when the notice is only late, not lost, and in the meantime your phone answered a commit request for the next deal that reached it on the connection the other device had already replaced, the commitment the other device made for your seat cannot be told from a forged one, and that hand fails its audit. If the server says it restarted, your phone starts its knowledge of the server's clock afresh, since a restarted server may keep time anew; a move the table then makes for you before you have moved or revealed again makes the hand amber, never green. A new match at the table is played on a new table with a clock of its own, so it starts that knowledge afresh too: nothing your phone learned of the last match's clock counts in it.
* **The table may not turn your moves away.** The server refuses only what is illegal in the state it is in, and every state it reached before a refusal has reached your phone first. At the audit your phone replays the hand and checks each refusal: if the move was legal in the state the server's own frames showed when it refused, the hand fails. A refusal of one of your moves names the turn the move was made on, as the server always does: one that names none is malformed and fails the hand, and a "not seated" answer, which the server sends only to a connection it seats no one on and which names no turn, excuses none of your moves (a move it answered counts as one the table never answered). On the connection the table seated your phone on, while your phone still holds the seat there (the server has neither opened it on another device nor taken it away, as a club's eviction does), "not seated" fails the hand on show: a server could otherwise drop a legal move, a declared win say, answer it that way and play the next move you make in its place. A refusal answers a move made in the hand on your screen, and your phone holds it against that hand whatever else the server has sent. An honest table answers your moves in the order they come: it plays each one, or refuses it naming its turn. Any other answer (another code, a refusal naming another turn, "not seated" with or without a turn, or nothing at all) leaves the move unanswered, and a legal move left so, followed on the same connection by another move of yours that the table played at that turn, fails the hand: a server cannot lose a win you declared and play your next move in its place. The table welcomes a connection before it sends anything about the game on it, and sends nothing about the game on a connection it took your seat away on (a club's eviction, after which your phone sends no move there): a frame about the game on such a connection fails the hand. A legal move that your phone sent over an unbroken link more than 30 seconds before the table made its own move for that turn, and that the table never answered, fails too. A move that reaches the table after your time ran out is honestly played on your next turn if it is still legal, and is not flagged.
* **Anchors.** The first hand of a match must start exactly where a new match starts under the table's rules (derived from the rules, not assumed). For a phone that sat down in the lobby this holds for the first hand it is dealt, whatever number the server gives it, except on a phone that sat down with neither a rejoin token nor a record, where a first hand after the match's first is amber with its starting scores unchecked (above). The bot seats and the rules are frozen at the match's first commit request: a later change of who is a bot, or of the rules, is flagged, and the restart bound and the bot check use the frozen set. A `welcome` that moves you to another seat or another match while the match is on is flagged and your seat stays where it was; a new match id is accepted only after the hands your phone verified say the match is over, and the new match must start where a new match starts: its first hand is hand 0, from the rules' starting scores. The server runs one match per table, so a new match at it is one your phone sits at from its lobby; one dealt a later hand first fails that hand.
* If the server cannot agree a deal (a fault on its side), it says so and returns to the lobby; a seated player's start, or the next tick while a player is still connected, deals the hand again. Players who saw the abandoned attempts will see that hand fail its audit.
* **Hands chain, one at a time.** After verifying a hand, your phone works out where it leaves the match (scores, dealer, dealer streak, rotations, prevailing wind, next hand number). The next deal must be that hand and start from exactly that state. From the moment your phone is asked to commit to a hand, or is shown it, until that hand's result arrives, the table has no business with any other hand: a commit request, state or result for another hand number fails the audit, so the server cannot drop a deal it does not like by moving on to the next number. A server that rewrites the scores between hands (also while your phone is reconnecting or restarting), skips a hand or deals a finished hand again fails the audit. A hand whose record does not replay fails, and nothing chains from it: the next hand is then amber, with the reason that the scores it started from could not be checked, and the final scores are not shown as verified. A result re-sent to a returning player must be the same audit, result and scores as before: a different one fails and never replaces what your phone verified, and an audit under another match id is treated as tampering.
* **Hands you missed.** While your link is down the table plays on, and once your seat's clock has run out the table moves for it. When your phone returns it tells the server which hand it last saw end, and the server sends the audit of every hand that ended since. Your phone checks each one as far as a device that was not there can: the shares and the seed, a replay that starts where the last hand it verified left the match, the restart accounting, whatever it did see of the hand before it left (its own share, the states it was shown, the moves it sent), and the clock: the moves the table made for your seat in those hands are held to the same rule as above, so a two-second absence cannot contain a timed-out turn, let alone the rest of a match. Your phone sent nothing in a hand it saw nothing of, so every move the record credits to your seat there is the table's, whatever the record calls it: a move written down as your own is held to the table's rule and to your time away like any other (unless you were away on purpose, or playing on from another device, when it may really have been yours). A hand that passes is amber (*your phone was away when this hand ended*) and the chain carries on through it, so the hand now on the table is checked as usual. Records that do not replay, do not follow from the last verified hand or skip a hand fail, and the hand on the table fails with them; a hand filed as "missed" for a phone that never left fails too. If the server sends no records at all, the next hand is amber with the reason that the scores it started from could not be checked. The hand your phone was on when its link dropped is then still listed, with a verdict of its own: amber (*your phone could not check this hand*), or red when your phone's join said which hands it held, since an honest server then sends the record of every hand that ended since before anything else, and keeps every record of the match, across a restart of the server too. A phone that came back without its record cannot say which hands it holds, and is sent none. A dishonest server could use the gap to change the scores; the amber note on the next hand is the most your phone can say about that.
* **Every frame is checked before it is used.** Your phone takes a frame from the table only when it carries what an honest server always sends in it, each figure of the right kind: a state's view with its turn number, its hash as text, its deadline as a number (or none) and its time bank as a number, and whether the time bank is running; a refusal of one of your moves with the turn the move was made on; a welcome with its rejoin token; the four seats as the table lists them; and so on for every kind of frame (`packages/net/src/intake.ts` lists each). A figure no clock gives, such as an infinity, is not a number of the right kind. A frame that falls short is malformed: your phone shows nothing of it, acts on nothing in it and files nothing from it, and the hand on show fails. An honest server never sends one. So everything your screen shows, and every move you make on it, is something your phone has filed and checks at the audit: a state shown without a turn number, a deadline sent as text or a state sent without its hash cannot slip past the checks of when your turn was shown or of the moves you made on it.
* **Every frame is about the hand on show.** Your phone files what a frame says under the hand it names, and an honest server names only the hand your phone is on, at the point where it sends each frame: a clock frame names the turn whose state it last sent you, the deal's commitments and stand-in notices name the hand being dealt, a refusal answers a move made in the hand on your screen, and a restart notice is the first word on a new connection. A frame naming any other hand is the server's own making: your phone makes no record from it, lets it change or push out nothing it keeps about the hand it is on, and fails that hand. Its records of each hand are kept for the last few hands, and never at the cost of the hand on show. The record it saves for an app restart holds exactly what it holds in memory: the same records of the same hands, under the same limits, so a restart forced at the right moment (a seat-taken notice, say) cannot drop a flag or a record the phone held.
* **The end of a match.** Your phone believes a `match_over` only when the last hand it verified ends the match under the rules and the final scores are exactly the ones the chain gives. Anything else is refused and shown in red, and the match stays pinned: the server cannot end a match early, invent final scores, or announce a fake end followed by a "new match" to make your phone forget a deal or a score. This holds after a dropped link as well, because an honest server first sends the hands you missed. Only a phone with no chain to follow (it came back without its record, or was never shown the hands it missed) shows the end without being able to check it, and the Fair play screen then says the final scores could not be checked. So does a phone that missed hands in which moves were made for its seat, or that came to a hand part-way through after moves were made for its seat in it: it checked those hands as far as it could, but it did not watch those moves, so the end is not shown as verified. The same holds when the phone picks the chain up again part-way through: the hands after the gap are checked and the end of the match is believed, but the scores those hands started from were not checked, so the final scores are still shown as unchecked. And when a hand of the match failed its check (red), the end is believed but its final scores are not shown as verified: your phone caught the table out in that hand, so the scores reached through it are the table's word. The final standings and the Fair play screen say so, in red. The board behind the final standings is the last one your phone was shown, which it held to that hand's replay. The board the end frame carries is checked by nothing, so it is shown only on a phone that holds none (one that came to the table at its end).
* **A server restart.** The server saves each table a moment after the fact, so after a crash it may resume a hand a few turns back, and those turns are played again, not necessarily the same way. A restored table tells every returning player which hand it resumed and at which turn (or that it resumed before the deal). Your phone then drops what it had been shown beyond that point, once per hand, and only what had reached it over a connection that has since been lost (a restart drops every connection). The hand is amber: *the server restarted during this hand and some turns were played again*. An orderly restart that lost nothing stays green. Turns played again without the notice, over a connection that never dropped, or after a second, different notice for the same hand fail, as any rewind does. A restarted table says so as its first word when your phone joins it again (after the records of any hands your phone missed), naming the hand it resumed, or the next hand when it was between hands; your phone takes the notice only there. A notice on a connection that has already shown play, or one for a hand before the one your phone is on, fails the hand your phone is on. The notice also starts your phone's knowledge of the server's clock afresh, and it does so once per hand: a second notice while the same hand is on show, even one naming another hand, fails that hand. A dishonest server could stage one such restart per hand to take back a few turns; the hand is then amber, never green.
* The audit (`seed`, all commitments, all secrets, the restarts, the full action log, the final state hash) is sent to every player. The client checks that it was asked to commit for this hand, that its own commitment and secret were recorded unchanged, that the commitments match the ones it was shown, that there is exactly one commitment and one secret per seat 0–4, every reveal against its commitment, the seed, a replay of the log through the engine (final hash, announced result and scores, its own final hand, every state and view it was shown, the turn each log entry names), the chaining, clock and refusal checks above and the ceremony accounting above.

## What your phone can and cannot check

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody picked the seed after seeing your share | Checked by your phone for each hand whose deal it watched whole, in one attempt, with its own share in the seed (**Verified**). Otherwise (a restart of the deal, a dropped link during the deal, a share the server made for you, joining mid-hand, a hand played while you were away, an app restart around the deal) the hand shows amber **Logged — the server could have picked this deal** and this claim rests on the server. |
| The server did not keep re-dealing until it liked the deal | Every restart makes the hand amber, and restarts are checked against what the human seats could have caused and against what your phone saw itself, across reconnects and app restarts. A re-deal that hides behind a dropped link or forged accounting fails; on a phone that came back without its saved record it cannot be shown to be a re-deal, so that first deal is amber. The server can still claim that other players' connections dropped (it cannot be told from a real drop), which is why such hands are amber, not green. |
| The server did not change the scores or the dealer between hands | Checked: the first hand must start where a new match starts, and each later hand where the previous hand, as replayed, left the match. The exception is stated on the hand: after hands your phone missed and was shown no records of, or after a hand whose record did not replay (that hand fails), the starting scores are marked as unchecked (amber). |
| The server did not skip a hand, deal one twice or abandon a deal by moving on | Checked: your phone is on one hand until its result arrives, and the next hand must be the one the last verified hand leads to. |
| The match ended when the rules say, with the scores the hands give | Checked: an end the verified hands do not lead to is refused and shown in red, and a new match id is accepted only after a verified end. A phone that holds no chain of hands (no saved record, or missed hands it was shown no records of) shows the end as unchecked, and a match in which a hand failed its check shows its final scores as not verified. |
| The server applied the rules correctly | Checked: replaying the published log must reproduce the final state, the announced result, the scores and every state your phone was shown, with the view of the table it showed you at each turn. |
| The server did not change or drop your moves | Checked: every move credited to you must be one you sent; a move the server refused must have been illegal in the state it showed; a legal move it neither played nor refused naming its turn, followed by another move of yours it played at that turn, fails; a legal move left unanswered for more than 30 seconds and replaced by the table's own fails (time your app was not running is left out of the 30 seconds). A move you sent in good time that the table plays over with a move of its own is amber, also when the connection it went out on dropped before the table's move came (a server that drops the move and then cuts the connection, or says the seat was opened on another device, loses it as a broken connection does): in good time means more than 1.5 seconds before the end of the clock your screen showed (by the end your screen counted to, which the table is held to, counted from when the turn's first state reached your phone: more than the best round trip of the hand plus 1.5 seconds, at most half the turn clock; a round trip is a time on your phone's own clock, so the best one measured before the move was sent counts, in any run of the app and across a restart the server announces; a move made after the app restarted, or the table was resumed from Home, during the turn counts from when the turn's state first reached the app again). **Honest cost:** a move sent just before your phone sleeps, the app goes to the background or the connection breaks, lost with that connection and played over by the table, makes its hand amber too (in simulated honest play, 99 hands in about 129,000, most of them where a connection was made to break as a move went out). **Not detectable:** a move you sent in the last 1.5 seconds your screen showed, which the server says arrived too late, looks the same as a slow network; and once the server says your seat was opened on another device, a move it credits to you from then until your phone holds the seat again cannot be told from one made there. |
| The server did not play your turn before your time ran out | Checked against the rules' turn clock and your time bank as your phone tracked it, not against the clock fields the server sends; a move the table makes for you must also never pass up a win. **How your phone times the table:** while it holds a seat your phone pings the table once a second, and right after each join; the table answers each at once with its clock. It pings, and watches the connection (below), whether or not the table has welcomed it on that connection: an honest table welcomes a connection before it sends anything else on it, so a server that sends the table but never the welcome turns none of these checks off (and the frames about the game it sends there fail the hand: below). Each answer ties the server's clock to your phone's own to within the round trip your phone timed. For each run of the server's clock, of the app and of your phone's own clock, your phone keeps the closest of these ties (with the deal's answer to your share) and measures every state against it: how much later than the tie allows the state reached it. Time the app was not running is left out. The ties are saved with the table's record, each with the run of the app it was made in and a check figure, so a turn shown late stays late after the app restarts or the table is resumed from Home: the states each run of the app saw are measured against that run's ties, as that run measured them. The record keeps what the last 32 runs of the app whose frames it holds carried, and in each hand the first ping of 16 runs (of the app, of the server's clock and of your phone's clock). **Limit:** a server that says your seat was opened on another device forces a resume, but each resume needs you to tap Resume on Home; one that had you do so 16 times in one hand, or 32 times across the hands on record, would push the oldest run's ties or first ping out of the record, and the turns that run was shown would then be measured only as an app that kept no ties measures them (17 seconds against the closest relation between the two clocks that run had seen). The record is saved a moment after each change, and at once when the server says your seat was opened on another device; an app stopped abruptly (the phone switched off, or the app closed by the system or by you) loses what reached it in the last half second or so before it stopped, and a turn shown in that time is not measured. A server has no ordinary way to stop the app: the app checks every frame before it uses it, and drops one it cannot read. The server's time stamps are believed up to 5 seconds beyond your phone's own clock (30 seconds until the table first answers a ping, which takes about a second; after the app restarts or the table is resumed from Home, a turn cut by more than 5 seconds before that makes the hand amber, not red), so a turn could be cut short by that much without failing (at Hyper, whose turn clock is 4 seconds, a whole turn; the allowance holds once per run of the app and of the server's clock, not on every turn). While a hand is played an honest server sends you the state of every turn, and a stamped clock frame with the turn whenever it has sent you nothing else for 3 seconds (its answers to your pings do not count). Each of these fails a hand in which the table then plays for you: a turn whose state never reached you on a connection that stayed open; a frame that arrives behind one stamped more than a second later; a clock frame for a turn whose state never came. The table's answers to your pings count among those frames, on every connection from its first frame: a state the table held back while it answered your pings (the deal's own state while your phone waits for it included) arrives behind answers stamped later than it, and when it shows you your turn, the hand fails also if you then play the turn yourself, as a turn shown late on a connection that stayed open does. That starts with the deal: on a connection that was up before the deal (one that brought you an earlier hand, or on which your phone committed to or revealed its share for this one), the first frame of the hand must be its deal's state, so a hand whose first frame there is a later turn, or its result, was held back. A clock frame names the turn whose state the server last sent you, or a later one when it held that state back: one for another hand, or for a turn before the one you were last shown, fails the hand you are on, whatever happens next, and your phone takes nothing else from it. An honest server stamps every state, so a turn's state that comes without its time stamp on a connection your phone already held fails the hand whether the table then plays for you or you move; on a new connection it counts as shown late. **The limit:** a turn or claim window that reaches your phone later than half its turn clock, and at least 2 seconds (15 seconds at Relaxed, 7.5 at Normal, 5 at Fast, 3.5 at Turbo, 2 at Hyper), makes the hand amber, never green. If the table then plays for you, its move is judged as one made while you had no timely view of the table: amber, or red when a whole clock cannot have run out. A table that answers none of your pings during a hand makes the hand amber. **The connection watch:** to watch the connection your phone keeps the closest relation it has seen between the server's stamps and its own clock, allowing at most half a second for the two clocks drifting apart since it was seen, however long ago, and uses the closest relation of the last 20 seconds (never closer than that of the whole run), so a connection that has grown slower, and stayed slow for 20 seconds, is not dropped over and over. When the server's stamps fall 8 seconds behind your phone's clock by the watch's measure, your phone asks the table for a time stamp, whatever frames keep arriving: stamps that creep forward a millisecond at a time do not count. After 5 more seconds it drops the connection and reconnects, so the time without frames is judged as a dropped connection (next row). The one connection it keeps then is one whose frames still come at the pace of their time stamps, the last within 4 seconds, behind by a delay that has held steady to within a second over the last 8 seconds: a delay that rose in one step and stayed up, as when a phone moves to a slower network, which the 20 seconds take in, and which may be too slow to connect again. The watch then starts measuring that connection afresh. These 3, 8, 5 and 20 seconds are fixed in your phone: a server can shorten the 3 and the 8 by announcing a shorter clock-frame period, never lengthen them. On a connection your phone already held, the state that shows you a new turn must give you the whole turn clock by its own deadline and stamp; and if the watch had been measuring that connection since before the state was 8 seconds late, the state must reach you within 17 seconds of its stamp by the watch's measure; otherwise the hand fails. Frames that pile up while the watch is not measuring (between hands, during the deal, while the app is in the background, and just after) are judged by the limit above, amber at worst, because an honest stall looks the same, unless they arrive out of the order of their stamps (above). **A turn first shown on a new connection:** your clock keeps running while a connection is down. A turn first shown on a new connection must leave you your whole clock less that limit, counted on your phone's own clock from when it arrived. The table playing for you sooner is amber (red when a whole clock cannot have run out), and so is a move of yours made when less than that was left. **Your screen's clock:** the clock on your screen counts the turn clock and then your bank by itself, from the state that started your turn, placed on your phone's clock by the closest tie. Until a ping is answered after the app restarts or your phone sleeps, it is placed by the quickest frame your phone has had since, so a turn the server holds on its way does not make it show more time. It never waits for the server to say the bank has started, and a wrong wall clock on your phone does not change it. It never counts to less than the rules' clock your phone tracked. A later end the server shows you binds it: if the table then plays for you before it, the hand fails, even if a later state took the time back. If the server says it restarted while you owed a move, and then plays that move for you, the hand is amber: the time your screen showed before the restart cannot be checked against the server's new clock. **Still not detectable:** a turn shown up to that limit late stays green, on every turn (the Fair play count, which measures your turns against the table's answers to your pings alone, counts each turn later than half of it: 7.5 seconds at Relaxed, 3.75 at Normal, 2.5 at Fast, 1.75 at Turbo, 1 at Hyper); a move made in the last 1.5 seconds your screen shows can be lost to the table's own move; and a server that runs its whole clock behind from the first frame your phone hears, answering your pings as if its clock were behind, looks like a fast connection to a clock that is behind. One answer to a ping sent at once with its stamp moved back, and everything after it late by the same amount, looks the same. Nothing your phone sees shows that, the count included. (Another answer sent that way, the deal say, moves the checks and your screen's clock alike, but the count still shows the turns late.) Because your screen counts the clock your phone holds the server to, such a server gains only the moves made in those last 1.5 seconds; a move made earlier that it plays over is amber. **Honest costs:** an honest stall or slow connection that delays a turn by more than that limit is amber too (at Hyper a 2-second stall as your turn starts is enough), and so are a reconnect that takes longer than the limit as a turn starts and a connection whose round trip is longer than it. A slow connection costs the moves you make in its last round trip, honest or not; your screen's clock ends that much sooner. A connection that grows slow during a match, toward the table only, is the exception: your screen's clock keeps to the quicker answers from before and does not end sooner, so a move you make in the last round trip arrives too late, and when the table plays over it the hand is amber (your phone cannot tell that from a server that holds your moves back). Your phone allows its clock and the server's to drift apart by 500 parts per million, and by half a second at most since the closest tie; real clocks drift less than half that. A phone clock that runs far faster, 2,000 parts per million say, makes its turns look later and later, so the later hands of a long match are amber: from about 20 minutes in at Hyper and about an hour in at Normal. One that runs that much slower makes the table's moves for you look early, and can fail such hands. After your phone sleeps, after the app restarts (your phone may close it while it is in the background), after your phone's clock is set forward by more than a second, or after a restart the server announces, the measure starts afresh from the next answered ping, within a second. Until then a turn is not measured against that limit; the turns shown before stay measured against the answers from before. A table that answers none of the pings your phone sends from then on cannot keep your turns out of it: a turn that reaches your phone more than that limit after the first of those pings, with none of them answered, is treated as a turn that reached your phone late (amber, and the table's move on it is judged as above). Pings lost with a connection that died while your phone slept count as unanswered too, so turns that waited in the network through the sleep can make that hand amber (mostly at Hyper, where the limit is 2 seconds). |
| The table played for a seat what its rule says, not a move of its choosing | Checked: every move the table made for a seat (a bot, a player out of time or away, a player who left) is recomputed from the replayed state and the published rule, and any other move fails the hand. **Limit:** a move the table made for another player may follow either of its two rules, since your phone cannot always tell whether that player dropped or left; a server that does not say how strong its bots are fails such hands (amber only on a phone restored from a save that predates the kept strength). |
| A dropped connection was not used to play your turn | Checked on your phone's own clock: the table may move for your seat while your link is down only when the time you were gone is long enough for your clock to have run out, and it may not take time from your bank beyond that. Too short fails the hand; long enough is amber with the reason. When the table passed a Pong, Chow or Kong you could have made while you were away, the hand names that claim, and the Fair play screen counts every such pass over the match: a server that cuts your link whenever you could claim, and lets the timeout rule pass it, cannot be told from bad luck in one hand, but it shows as a count that keeps rising. A turn first shown to you on a new connection, with less than its whole clock, less half its turn clock (at least 2 seconds), before the table plays for you, is judged as a dropped connection from the last frame your phone believed (amber, or red when a whole clock cannot have run out since), and so is a turn shown so late that its time stamp is not believed. Such a turn is named on the hand as one that reached your phone late, not as time your connection was down (it may never have dropped), and the claims the table passed on such turns are counted apart on the Fair play screen. A hand your phone was shown nothing of before the table's first move for you in it (its connection dropped right after it sent its share, say, or the first it hears of the hand is its result) is judged the same way, from the last frame it held before; and that move needed a whole clock, the turn clock and the full time bank every deal starts with, after your phone sent its share for the deal, by your phone's own clock: sooner fails the hand. A drop is measured from the last state your phone held, never from a clock frame or a pong, and a clock frame or a pong does not make a new connection count as working either: only a state or a hand's end that reaches you on it in time does. **Limit:** the same 5 seconds (30 until the table answers a ping, which it does within a second of a join or of a restart it announces; a move it makes for you before then is amber); the time without a connection is not judged where you left the table yourself, the seat was opened on another device, or the app restarted in between, though the states your phone was shown before that still are. One honest case still shows red: your phone is offline, the same account opens the seat on another device and leaves the table there (the table then plays the seat at once), and your phone comes back before your clock would have run out. It never saw the takeover, so it judges the gap as if the table had played early. |
| Hands played while you were away were dealt and scored honestly | Partly checked: the server's record of each missed hand must have matching shares and seed, replay under the rules and chain from the last hand your phone verified, and the moves made for your seat must fit in the time you were away and follow the table's rule, even where the record calls them your own. Your phone did not watch the deal or the play, so these hands are amber, and a match end reached through a missed hand with moves for your seat is not shown as verified. **Not detectable:** when you were away on purpose or played on from another device, a move the record credits to you cannot be told from the real thing on the phone that was away; those hands stay amber. |
| A hand replayed after a server crash is the hand you saw | Not fully checkable: the turns played again replace what your phone was shown. The hand is amber with the reason, once per hand and only with the server's notice and a lost connection; anything else fails. |
| The server did not look at the wall during the hand | **Not checked.** The server knows the seed from the deal onwards. Mitigation: the planned cryptographic shuffle (below). |
| The seats the table calls players are people | **Not checked.** The server decides which seats are human: each seat comes with a name and a flag saying whether it is a bot, and your phone takes that flag as given (it fixes it from the match's first deal, so a seat cannot switch mid-match). Only the seats the table itself calls bots, and the moves it says it made for a seat, are recomputed. A bot the server presents as a human seat, with a name, plays moves your phone treats as that player's own and never recomputes, so such a bot could read every hand and your phone would not know; this holds at quick match, at a club table and anywhere else the server seats players. The deal and the replay are still checked for those hands. |
| The server did not leak the wall to another player | **Not checked.** The server sees every wall by design and could pass one to a player. Mitigations this release: one seat per account at a table (a second seat for the same account is refused, so one account cannot see two hands), and quick match never seats two players who have blocked each other. Nothing in the audit can show a leak. |
| Another player did not see your hand | Enforced by the server: each seat receives only its own view (no other hand, no other seat's drawn tile, claim options or concealed kong; while a discard can be claimed the table lists every other seat as still to answer, so it never shows who could pong, kong or win on it). This relies on the server being honest; nothing in the audit shows it. Two players colluding over another channel are not detected. |

## Publishing and testing the generator

In priority order: a plain-language and technical spec page (this document plus `packages/engine/src/rng.ts`); a downloadable per-hand audit from the result screen; a standalone open-source verifier (CLI and browser) that rebuilds the wall from an audit, in the spirit of Tenhou's public wall reproducers; the engine and protocol under an OSI licence; and a monthly tile-statistics report (per-tile draw frequencies, dealer win rate, win-type distribution with chi-square p-values across public hands). Before buying a certificate, run and publish the open batteries on the DRBG output (Dieharder, NIST SP 800-22, TestU01 Crush/BigCrush) plus a GLI-style "range 1–144, 144 selections" draw file; then budget a GLI-19-track RNG certificate (4–8 weeks, covers the RNG/shuffle only) once revenue or any prize context justifies it.

## Roadmap to zero-trust dealing

* **Mental mahjong shuffle** (later, opt-in "paranoid mode"): elliptic-curve SRA so every draw is decrypted cooperatively and no device, host included, learns a tile before its owner. Cost is roughly 430 point multiplications and ~55 KB per player per hand plus a round trip per draw, and no known protocol tolerates a player dropping mid-hand — which conflicts with crash-resume. Ship only once key escrow for disconnected players is solved. Until then online tables run on the game server, which is trusted not to look at the wall; offline games against bots run on your phone.
* **Third-party RNG and engine audit**: publish the engine, the seed derivation and a statistical report on millions of simulated hands (`npm run sim`) so an independent lab can certify the distribution. Simulation today: win rates by seat within noise of 25% each, draw rate ~4%, no seat bias.
* **Signed audits**: hosts sign each audit so disputes can be settled offline by anyone with the public key.

## What players see

* A green **✓ Shuffle & log verified** badge on every result screen when your phone watched the whole deal in one attempt with your own share in it; amber **Logged — the server could have picked this deal** (no shield) with the reason when everything checked out but the server could have chosen the deal or your phone did not watch all of the hand (a restart of the deal, the app or the server, a dropped link, hands played while you were away, one of your turns reaching your phone late, the table playing over a move you sent in good time, a table that did not answer your phone's timing checks); red **⚠ Audit failed** with the reason if anything is off.
* A **Fair play** screen listing each hand's seed, commitments, secrets, action count and hash, for the last 50 hands at this table (kept across reconnects and app restarts), and, over the match, how many claims the table passed for you while your connection was down or on a turn that reached you late, and how many of your turns reached you later than half the time a turn may be late (a quarter of the turn clock, at least 1 second). These counts are never a verdict.
* Planned, not yet true: the engine and the protocol published under an open licence, with a standalone verifier (see "Publishing and testing the generator"). No licence has been chosen yet and all rights are reserved (`docs/OWNER_TODO.md`).
